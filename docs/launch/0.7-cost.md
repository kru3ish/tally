**First comment:**

Hi HN. I kept finishing a story with Claude Code, looking at the bill, and having no idea whether $75 was a lot for that story or why. Tally answers both questions while you are still in the session.

1. Paste the Jira/GitHub/Linear link (or a .md file, or text) as usual. Tally compares it with every session already on your disk (TF-IDF over the ticket or first prompts, the similarity-weighted median of the closest ones' cost) and tells you at the end of the first turn: "stories like this have cost ~$50 here (typical $38–$65, from 6 similar past stories)". No model call, nothing leaves the machine.
2. The status line shows `$31 / ~$50 est`.
3. When spend crosses the estimate, the Stop hook tells you in the UI, in that session: how far over, and the biggest lever with its one-line fix.
4. `/tally:cost` shows where the money went, each driver priced from the transcript: the main loop on Opus (same tokens repriced at Sonnet), subagents inheriting an expensive model, failing commands retried, files re-read, MCP servers loaded and never used, one long conversation re-sending 80k+ tokens every call.
5. `tally fix model-sonnet` (or `subagent-sonnet`, `claude-md`, `drop-unused-mcp`) writes the change into the repo's `.claude/settings.json` or CLAUDE.md; commit it and the whole team has it. `tally undo` reverts byte for byte.

On one of my real sessions, a $156.52 game build on Opus: $55 of it was the model (same tokens at Sonnet prices), $52 was re-sending a long context 207 times, $39 was subagents inheriting Opus. Up to $114.60 avoidable combined, with the overlaps counted once.

How good is the estimate? `tally estimate --backtest` prices each of your past sessions from all the others. On my 169 sessions: median error 40%, 70% within 2× of the actual cost. That's "about this much", not a quote, and it gets better as history grows. With nothing similar it says so and never calls a story "over" against unrelated work.

Things I expect pushback on:
- The model saving is an upper bound: Sonnet may take more turns. The output says so.
- The estimate is nearest-neighbour on words, not a model. That keeps it free, instant and reproducible. I'd like to hear whether a model would beat it on your history.

MIT, TypeScript, local. `npm i -g @kru3ish/tally && tally install`, or `/plugin marketplace add kru3ish/tally`. Start with `tally estimate --backtest` to see how it would have priced your own history.

## Short post (LinkedIn / X)

Coding agents tell you what a session cost. They don't tell you what the story *should* have cost, or why it went over.

Tally 0.7 puts a price on a Jira/GitHub story from your own past sessions before the agent starts. It tells you in the same session when you go over, shows where the money went (model, subagents, retries, unused MCP servers, long context), and applies the fix in one line to the repo's Claude Code settings, so the whole team gets it.

One real session: $156 on Opus, up to $114 avoidable. Backtest on 169 of my sessions: median error 40%, 70% within 2×.

Local, no model calls, MIT: github.com/kru3ish/tally
